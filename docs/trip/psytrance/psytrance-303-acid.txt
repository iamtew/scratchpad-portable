303 ACID

The bass toks.
Two of these roll.

One saw. One voice. Each is monophonic.
The lead is the squelch in the sections below. The driver is the last section.

It sits two octaves above the bass root, and it follows that root every 8 bars.
The kick and the bass keep the sub.
The squelch lives in the mids.

==================================================
FILTER
==================================================

Resonance stays high.
That is the wet edge. Leave it there. Do not ride it per note.

Cutoff does the squelch.

A new note opens the filter in a few milliseconds, then the cutoff falls.
Decay is short. An accent opens further and falls a little slower.
That chirp is the squelch. Not every note gets one.

A slide glides the pitch across one 16th.
The glide does not retrigger the filter. The pitch smears through whatever the filter is already doing.
An accent still chirps, even when the pitch slid into it.
The leap up into the octave is the one to listen for.

The env mod is the ride. It climbs and falls over 32 bars.
Low on that ride, the chirp is small. At the top, the same notes scream.
An accent still opens further, wherever the ride is.
The cutoff itself only drifts a little, plus the slow wobble under the envelope.
The knob keeps cycling. The climax does not pin it open.

==================================================
PATTERN
==================================================

Sixteen steps. Mostly 16ths. Two rests.

The rests are there so the decay can be heard.
Accents sit off the kick, on steps 1, 6, 9, and 14.
Slides never run into a rest.

0   root, slide up
1   octave, accent
2   7, slide
3   3
4   rest
5   root, slide
6   3, accent
7   7, slide
8   10
9   octave, accent
10  rest
11  7, slide
12  3
13  root, slide
14  5, accent
15  7, let it die

Four short gestures.
It drives. It does not solo.

==================================================
SATURATION
==================================================

Saw, then the resonant lowpass, then the shaper, then a highpass.

The resonant peak is what gets fat.
The shaper is hotter than the lead. It is oversampled.
Hotter than that and the resonance turns into a buzz. Stop before the buzz.

Highpass after the shaper, around 180 Hz.
New harmonics do not get to sit on the kick.

A little of the line goes to the delay.
The delay return is already highpassed.

One side of the dry note is a few milliseconds late.
That is the width. It is still one saw.

==================================================
DRIVING
==================================================

The other saw. Same octave. It ticks under the lead.

Sixteen steps, all of them notes.
du is the root. da is the fifth. dee is the octave.

du du du du du da du du du du du du dee da du da

No rests. No slides. Every note retriggers.
The fifths accent, on steps 5, 13, and 15. Those sit off the kick.
The octave is step 12. That step is the kick, so it does not chirp.

The ride is the same 32 bars, half a cycle later.
When the lead is open, this one is dull.
The chirp is short. It ticks. It does not scream.

Resonance stays lower. The shaper stays cooler.
Highpass a little higher, so it does not sit on the lead.
Almost none of it goes to the delay.
It stays in the center. The lead keeps the width.
Quieter than the lead.

==================================================
WHERE IT PLAYS
==================================================

Not in the intro.
Not in the breakdown.

The kick and the bass have to be obvious before either line starts.
Both use that gate. The driver stays under the lead.
